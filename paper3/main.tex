\begin{abstract}
Verified search --- a language model proposing programs, a hard evaluator
scoring them, selection keeping the best --- is how machine systems have
recently moved mathematical records. The published systems vary the
scaffolding (islands, ensembles, novelty machinery) and report the wins; none
measures, with controls, which parts of the \emph{proposer's cognition}
matter. We instrument a minimal FunSearch-style loop at office scale (a
30B-parameter local model on a laptop, 120--600 verified samples per run)
with three operators isolated in our earlier work on open-ended generation
\citep{ono2026interrupting}: a \emph{schematic notebook} the model writes
and carries instead of verbatim elite programs, a \emph{named obstacle}
(agenda), and \emph{behavioural repulsion} from constructions already found.
On six construction problems from the AlphaEvolve repository
\citep{georgiev2025exploration}, a pre-registered $5\times6$ factorial with
a replicate shows: the composed operators improve the fraction of the
seed-to-record gap closed in 5 of 6 problems (+0.20 pooled, $p=0.0625$ at
the $n{=}6$ exactness floor); behavioural repulsion raises functional
diversity at the minimum attainable $p$ ($+0.38$, $p=0.0156$) and collapses
without the anchoring memory (three collapses alone, none composed \todo{check});
and at larger budgets the composition scales while repulsion alone
plateaus. Swapping the proposer for a frontier model (Claude Opus~5) under
the same loop closes in tens of samples what the local model cannot close
in hundreds, and exceeds the value AlphaEvolve reported on the
beat-the-average problem (0.3897 vs 0.3890) with 300 samples; tripling the
verifier's time budget moves this by $+0.0002$. Across proposers, grids and
time budgets the search stalls at $\approx$92\% of the best known value,
whose record construction evidently lies outside the sparse-atom family
every proposer writes: the remaining mile is an idea, not a budget. We
release the harness, every candidate, and the pre-registrations.
\end{abstract}
