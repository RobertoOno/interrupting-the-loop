\begin{abstract}
Evaluating thousands of tokens of model-generated text with an LLM judge
that reads one window at a time is common practice. In a study of
interventions on long, open-ended generation by base language models, judged
by Claude Opus~5 on 96- to 160-token windows with 600 tokens of context, we
found three things the judge could not see. Each was large enough to change
the conclusions. (1)~Text the experimenter injects into a stream (a new
subject every few hundred tokens) ends up inside the judged windows and is
scored as the model's own; a reported growth of the effect with the period
was mostly the injected sentence. (2)~Given the same four injected sentences
in rotation, a base model replays its own earlier segments from 600 to 1,200
tokens back, beyond the judge's horizon. At periods 150--300, 65--80\% of
post-interruption windows were more than half verbatim reproductions, and
the judge scored them as surprise and, worse, as connection. (3)~Local gains
do not compose. On whole documents no condition built an integrated text,
and the condition with the best windows read as a sequence of restarts. We
describe the protocol that corrects these: windows of generated text only,
fresh-only estimates with a shingle-based self-copy flag, a document-level
reading, the premise rather than the window as the unit of inference, a
pipeline test--retest from two byte-identical arms, a second judge family and
human readers. We also report what survives it. The intervention's effect on
surprise is real and about one point smaller than first reported; its effect
on connection was mostly artefact. The lesson is general: a windowed judge
measures what is in the window, and what a long stream does is partly
outside it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The underlying study \citep{ono2026interrupting} asks where the novelty a
base language model produces with no task comes from. A cell is one
premise (a single sentence) continued by a base model (mainly
Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, 8-bit, MLX) for 4,500 tokens with the end-of-text token
masked; arms differ in whether a windowed repetition penalty
(\emph{habituation}) is on and in whether, how often and with what the
stream is \emph{interrupted}: a short sentence injected into the model's
cache as if it had written it, such as \emph{``Meanwhile, in a city with no
name,''}, every 150 to 900 tokens. Ten premises are shared by all arms;
24 arms were run. Windows of the stream are judged by Claude Opus~5 on three
0--10 dimensions (surprise given the earlier text, connection to it,
coherence), five calls per window with the median as the score, the 600
preceding tokens shown as context. The judge was measured: intra-window
spread 0.45--0.70, agreement with a second family (Kimi K2.6) $\rho=0.65$ to
$0.85$, agreement of three human raters' consensus with the judge
$\rho=0.58$ on surprise. The first version of the study, written under this
protocol, reported that the interruption raised surprise from 1.7 to 3.1 and
connection from 1.1 to 3.2 over habituation, that its yield grew with the
length of the thread it broke (5.3--5.9 after 300--900 tokens against 3.1
after 150), and that a period of about 300 tokens was best. Each of these
numbers was partly an artefact of what the judge could and could not see.

\section{Three things the judge could not see}

\paragraph{1. The injected text.} The first protocol cut 160-token windows
right before each injection and on a uniform grid, so that a window after an
interruption contained the injected sentence. The judge, asked how
unexpected the window is given the earlier text, read the experimenter's
sentence as the model's turn. Re-judging with windows of generated text only
(96 tokens starting 32 tokens after the end of the injected sentence, none
crossing an injection; uniform grid in uninterrupted arms) kept the direction
of every effect and removed the period story entirely: the post-interruption
window scores 3.0 at period 150, 2.9 at 300, 2.9 at 600 and 3.2 at 900, every
paired difference covering zero. Across 14 conditions the means under the
two protocols correlate at $\rho=+0.82$ (surprise) to $+0.94$ (connection);
the sizes differ exactly where an injected sentence sat in the window.

\paragraph{2. Self-copy beyond the horizon.} A base model given the same
four subject-change sentences in rotation learns the rotation: after
\emph{``That night she dreamed of something else entirely:''} it tends to
write what it wrote the last time that sentence appeared, 600 or 1,200 tokens
earlier. We flag a judged window as \emph{copied} when at least half of its
12-token shingles occur earlier in the same stream, and record whether the
source lies within the 600 tokens the judge sees (Table~\ref{tab:copy}). Bare
generation is 67\% copied (literal loops, all visible to the judge, who
scores them 0.0); habituation alone 27\%; the interruption arms with the
fixed rotation 65--80\% at periods 150 and 300, and at 300 the source of 62\%
of all windows lies outside the judge's context; 38\% at 600, 12\% at 900;
the arm whose context is reset at each interruption 0\%. A copied window
scores high on ``connection'' for the obvious reason (it \emph{is} earlier
text) and, when its source is out of view, on surprise. On fresh windows
only, the interruption still adds +1.2 \ci{+0.4}{+2.1} of surprise at period
150 and +1.3 at 300 over habituation ($p\le0.01$, exact paired permutation
over ten premises), but its connection effect falls from +2.4 to +0.75; the
reset arm, which cannot replay, adds +1.9 and +2.0. We found this because a
document-level judge (below) remarked that the stream ``repeats the same
four-part block verbatim''.

\paragraph{3. Windows and wholes.} Every score above is local. We had the
judge read the whole 4,500-token stream of each cell, injected sentences
removed, and score integration (parts taken up and joined later),
development (something builds rather than restarts), coherence and surprise
once per document ($k=3$). No condition exceeds 2.5 on any dimension. The
interrupted stream over the preserved context, whose windows are the best,
reads \emph{below} the uninterrupted habituated one (surprise 0.1 vs 1.4,
$p=0.002$; development 0.3 vs 1.1): a sequence of restarts. The reset arm
reads best (2.0 / 1.6 / 2.2 / 2.5), because each of its parts is a clean
piece that returns to the premise; this replicated on ten new premises.

\section{The protocol that corrects them}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \textbf{Windows of generated text only}, starting a margin after any
injected text and never crossing an injection; the context shown to the
judge may contain injected text, the graded region never does.
\item \textbf{A self-copy flag} per window (shingles seen earlier in the
stream; source within or beyond the judge's horizon) and \textbf{fresh-only
estimates} reported alongside the all-windows ones; a design rule that
follows: never inject the same sentence twice, or reset the context.
\item \textbf{A document-level reading} of the whole stream, with injected
text removed, as the check that local gains compose.
\item \textbf{The stream, not the window, as the unit of inference}: cell
means, bootstrap intervals over cells, exact sign-flip permutation tests on
paired cells, effect sizes on cell means; window-level statistics only as
descriptive.
\item \textbf{A measured judge}: the median of $k$ calls; the intra-window
spread; a pipeline test--retest (two arms that turned out byte-identical in
all ten cells were judged independently: medians of five agree exactly in
91\% of windows on surprise, cell means differ by $-0.01$
\ci{-0.09}{+0.07}); a second judge family on a random, not judge-stratified,
sample ($\rho=0.75$ / $0.80$ / $0.65$ on surprise / connection /
coherence); and human raters on fresh windows.
\item \textbf{A pre-registered confirmatory battery} on new premises and a
new random seed, analyzed under the protocol fixed in advance: the primary
contrast confirmed ($p=0.003$; on fresh windows $p=0.04$), the expectation
that a kept context carries connection refuted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What survives}

The intervention is real and smaller than first reported. A new subject
injected every few hundred tokens into a habituated stream raises judged
surprise on fresh generated text by about 1.2 to 1.4 points over habituation
alone, on three base models from two families, a post-trained model, an
unquantized model and a second genre. A bare paragraph break adds nothing
detectable on fresh text, and a connective that asks for continuity hurts.
The kept context is not what produces the effect, and the connection it
seemed to carry was mostly replay. What did not survive were the claims that
depended on what the judge could not see. We think the general point holds
for any evaluation of long generation with a windowed LLM judge. The judge
measures what is in the window, and a long stream does much of what it does
outside it, including copying the experimenter and copying itself. Code,
data, prompts and the analysis are in the repository of
\citet{ono2026interrupting}.
