\section{Conclusion}

Where does the novelty a base language model can produce with no task come
from? Not, at the resolution we could reach, from the sampler: an
anti-probable decoder buys surface novelty that shows no detectable effect at
the level of ideas. Not, under the operationalizations we tested, from the
prompt. It comes from the loop, and from a simple part of it. A base model
feeding on its own output degenerates; damp its literal repetition and
interrupt it, every few hundred tokens, with a sentence that leads somewhere
new, and its generated text is judged more surprising and somewhat more
connected, by an LLM judge from another family and by human readers, on
windows that contain none of the injected words and none of the stream's own
earlier text. What carries it is the new subject, with or without the
earlier text in the model's memory; a boundary alone does nothing; a
pre-registered replication on new premises confirms the main contrast and
refutes the one about memory; and the gains do not compose into a whole:
read as documents, these streams remain collections, and a Review that
decides when not to interrupt does not change that.

The other half of what we learned is about measurement. A windowed LLM judge
of a long stream scores the experimenter's injected sentence as the model's
own, scores the model's replay of its own earlier segments as surprise and
connection when the source lies beyond its horizon, and cannot tell a local
gain from a whole. Generated-only windows, fresh-only estimates, a
document-level reading, the premise as the unit and a measured judge are
what it took to see this; we suspect they are what it takes in general. What
we have characterized is a controllable intervention on forced open-ended
generation and the instrument needed to characterize it honestly. Whether
interruption serves a model at work on a problem, where a verifier replaces
the judge and a find can be kept because it is worth something, is the
question this study leaves open and the next one asks.
