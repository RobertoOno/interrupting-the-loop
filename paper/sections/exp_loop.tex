\section{The loop}
\label{sec:loop}

All results in this section use Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base unless stated, ten
premises, 4,500 tokens per cell, $\lambda=0$, Claude Opus~5 as judge with
$k=5$, generated-only windows, and the cell as unit: numbers are means over
cells (one per premise), intervals are 95\% bootstrap CIs over cells, and
paired differences come with an exact sign-flip permutation $p$ on the ten
paired cells and Cliff's $\delta$ on cell means. Full tables, including the
Benjamini--Hochberg $q$-values within each family of comparisons and the
event-protocol tables of the first version, are in Appendix~\ref{app:loop}.
Every figure shows one point per premise.

\subsection{Forced continuation degenerates; the scaffold revives it}

Left to continue a premise with nothing else, the base model degenerates
within a few hundred tokens into a repetitive or corpus-like mode and stays
there: \emph{``Highly recommended. Highly recommended.\,\ldots''} for two
thousand tokens; a table of three-digit numbers; an English-exam answer key.
Judged surprise 0.48 \ci{0.30}{0.72}, connection 0.33, coherence 2.56 (ten
cells). The full scaffold brings the stream to 2.89 / 1.93 / 5.91, and every
premise moves in the same direction (Figure~\ref{fig:q1}). But the scaffold
is not the smallest thing that does this.

\subsection{Taking the loop apart: habituation and interruption}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{The ladder.} Two operations of the scaffold suffice, and they are
not the elaborate ones (Figure~\ref{fig:q1}, Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). Adding
only \emph{habituation} to bare generation (a windowed repetition penalty, no
interruption) removes the literal orbits: surprise 0.48 $\rightarrow$ 1.30
($\Delta$ +0.81 \ci{+0.21}{+1.42}, $p=0.06$), coherence 2.56 $\rightarrow$
4.52; the stream is fluent and, to the judge, still not surprising. Adding a
neutral subject change every 150 tokens over the preserved context takes it
to 3.04 / 3.72 / 6.20: over habituation alone, surprise $\Delta$ +1.54
\ci{+0.69}{+2.46} ($p=0.008$, $\delta=+0.75$), connection +2.58
\ci{+1.58}{+3.69} ($p=0.008$, $\delta=+1.00$: every premise), coherence
+1.67 \ci{-0.29}{+3.40}. This plain interruption matches the full scaffold on
surprise (3.04 vs 2.89) and beats it on connection (3.72 vs 1.93) and
coherence (6.20 vs 5.91): the salience monitor, the in-loop judge (which
never fired) and the forgetting add nothing detectable over the interruption,
and forgetting costs connection. Habituation accounts for part
of the surprise; the interruption accounts for the rest and for most of the
additional connection beyond habituation.

\paragraph{Where the stream goes.} In sentence-embedding space
(Figure~\ref{fig:traj}) bare generation has explored radius 0.18 and mean step
0.14 and ends 0.78 from the premise; the scaffold has radius 0.51 and returns
to within 0.57 of the premise; the clock reseed spreads over the space in
jumps while keeping the whole context, which is where its connection
advantage comes from.

\subsection{The interruption must lead away}
\label{sec:content}

With the same period (150), a neutral subject change and a re-encounter
stitch that asks the stream to return to its beginning are indistinguishable
(3.04 / 3.72 / 6.20 vs 2.94 / 3.96 / 5.44; every paired CI covers zero).
Injecting the \emph{premise itself} (1.29 / 1.01 / 3.96) or a 64-token window
of the stream's \emph{own past} (0.90 / 1.05 / 3.54) is as bad as not
interrupting at all: against the neutral change, surprise $-1.64$
\ci{-2.15}{-1.15} and $-1.89$ \ci{-2.69}{-1.01}, connection $-2.62$ and
$-2.43$, coherence $-2.21$ and $-2.54$, all $p\le0.016$, $\delta$ $-0.84$ to
$-1.00$ (Figure~\ref{fig:q2}). Qualitatively, the own-past injection
reinforces whatever mode the stream is in: an exam-key well was fed its own
questions back. A return works only as a short stitch that still opens a new
sentence; a literal return closes the loop.

\subsection{Timing: salience is a good reader and a bad metronome}
\label{sec:timing}

The stitch injected on salience events (jump, crystallization, recurrence;
1--9 per cell, median $\approx$5) yields post-interruption windows as good as
the same stitch on a clock of matched frequency (every 900 tokens): 3.38 /
2.71 / 5.55 vs 3.47 / 3.11 / 6.19, paired differences within $\pm0.5$ and CIs
covering zero (Figure~\ref{fig:q4}). What differs is the stream between
interruptions. Salience events fire rarely and unevenly and leave stretches
of thousands of tokens uninterrupted; on windows at least 300 generated
tokens after the last injection, the salience-timed cells score 1.65 / 1.61 /
4.29 against 2.98 / 1.80 / 5.12 for the clocked stitch and 2.58 / 1.84 /
5.38 for the neutral change every 900 (Table~\ref{tab:gen-q4b}). Salience
events select good moments to look at, which is why salience-gated review
beat clock review as a policy for which windows to judge in the first
version of this study; as an interruption trigger, they under-interrupt.
Salience should decide where to look, not when to interrupt. \todo{refresh
numbers when battery 2 is complete}

\subsection{Period and decay}
\label{sec:period}

The first version of this study reported that the yield of an interruption
grew with the length of the thread it broke (5.3--5.9 after 300--900-token
threads against 3.1 after 150) and decayed over the next few hundred tokens.
With the injected sentence excluded from the judged window, that finding
largely disappears (Figure~\ref{fig:q5}). The post-interruption window
(32--128 generated tokens after the injection) scores 3.04 at period 150,
2.85 at 300, 3.33 at 600 and 2.53 at 900 on surprise, with every paired
difference against 150 covering zero; connection is highest at the shortest
period (3.72 vs 2.13, 2.94 and 1.73; $\Delta$ $-1.5$ to $-1.8$, $p\le0.03$)
and coherence rises slightly with the period (6.20 $\rightarrow$ 6.43, 6.73,
6.93). Deeper into a segment the surprise of generated text decays only
mildly (period 900: 2.53, 2.53, 3.17, 2.33, 3.00 and 1.40 at 32, 160, 300,
450, 600 and 750 tokens after the injection), so that the stream estimate
(offset means weighted by the stretch of the segment they represent) is 3.04
/ 3.72 at 150, 2.48 / 2.04 at 300, 3.05 / 2.64 at 600 and 2.49 / 1.87 at
900. Among the tested periods, then, no period is better than 150 on the
generated text itself, and the shortest period gives the most connection;
what the longer periods buy is a little coherence. A period of 75 cannot be
measured under this protocol (no 96-token window of generated text fits
between two injections); under the event protocol its stream was dead
(0.88), which is the point: nothing develops. The larger numbers of the first
version were, in good part, the judge reading the injected sentence. What
survives is the direction of every effect and the operating range: a break
every 150--600 tokens over the preserved context. \todo{refresh numbers when
battery 2 is complete}

\subsection{Three generator models from two families}
\label{sec:families}

The ladder replicates on Qwen3-8B-Base and OLMo-2-13B (Table~\ref{tab:ladder},
Figure~\ref{fig:families}). On the 8B: 0.43 $\rightarrow$ 1.37 $\rightarrow$
2.76 (interruption over habituation: surprise +1.39 \ci{+0.72}{+2.07},
$p=0.008$; connection +2.17 \ci{+1.53}{+2.83}, $p=0.002$), scaffold 2.70 /
2.35. On OLMo, whose bare stream wanders across web genres rather than
locking into literal orbits and is therefore less dead (1.41), the
interruption over habituation gains connection (+1.51 \ci{+0.73}{+2.32},
$p=0.008$) and only some surprise (+0.67 \ci{-0.40}{+1.73}, $p=0.29$), and
costs nothing detectable in coherence; there the full scaffold is the best
arm on surprise (3.34, +1.20 over habituation, $p=0.08$) and coherence (6.30,
+1.95, $p=0.03$): its selective forgetting lifts the stream out of a
web-boilerplate well, the mechanism the calibration probes had suggested,
at the price of connection (2.04 vs 2.91). Whether forgetting is worth its
connection cost depends on how deep the generator's well is. \todo{refresh
OLMo numbers when its judging is complete}
