\section{The loop}
\label{sec:loop}

All results in this section use Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base unless stated, ten
premises, 4,500 tokens per cell, $\lambda=0$, Claude Opus~5 as judge with
$k=5$, generated-only windows, and the cell as unit: numbers are means over
cells (one per premise), intervals are 95\% bootstrap CIs over cells, and
paired differences come with an exact sign-flip permutation $p$ on the ten
paired cells and Cliff's $\delta$ on cell means. Full tables, including the
Benjamini--Hochberg $q$-values within each family of comparisons and the
event-protocol tables of the first version, are in Appendix~\ref{app:loop}.
Every figure shows one point per premise.

\subsection{Forced continuation degenerates; the scaffold revives it}

Left to continue a premise with nothing else, the base model degenerates
within a few hundred tokens into a repetitive or corpus-like mode and stays
there: \emph{``Highly recommended. Highly recommended.\,\ldots''} for two
thousand tokens; a table of three-digit numbers; an English-exam answer key.
Judged surprise 0.45 \ci{0.28}{0.68}, connection 0.30, coherence 2.52 (ten
cells). The full scaffold brings the stream to 2.70 / 1.85 / 6.02, and every
premise moves in the same direction (Figure~\ref{fig:q1}). But the scaffold
is not the smallest thing that does this.

\subsection{Taking the loop apart: habituation and interruption}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{The ladder.} Two operations of the scaffold suffice, and they are
not the elaborate ones (Figure~\ref{fig:q1}, Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). Adding
only \emph{habituation} to bare generation (a windowed repetition penalty, no
interruption) removes the literal orbits: surprise 0.45 $\rightarrow$ 1.55
($\Delta$ +1.10 \ci{+0.70}{+1.40}, $p=0.004$, $\delta=+0.80$), connection
0.30 $\rightarrow$ 1.28, coherence 2.52 $\rightarrow$ 4.48; the stream is
fluent and, to the judge, still not very surprising. Adding a neutral subject
change every 150 tokens over the preserved context takes it to 3.02 / 3.68 /
6.12: over habituation alone, surprise $\Delta$ +1.47 \ci{+0.95}{+2.08}
($p=0.002$, $\delta=+0.87$), connection +2.40 \ci{+1.58}{+3.32} ($p=0.002$,
$\delta=+0.93$), coherence +1.63 \ci{+0.23}{+2.87} ($p=0.06$). This plain
interruption matches the full scaffold on surprise (3.02 vs 2.70) and beats
it on connection (3.68 vs 1.85; the scaffold's gain over habituation is +0.57
\ci{+0.17}{+1.02}) at equal coherence (6.12 vs 6.02): the salience monitor,
the in-loop judge (which never fired) and the forgetting add nothing
detectable over the interruption, and forgetting costs connection.
Habituation accounts for part of the surprise; the interruption accounts for
the rest and for most of the additional connection beyond habituation.

\paragraph{The factorial and the baselines.} Battery~3 completes the
$2\times2$: the neutral subject change \emph{without} habituation reaches
2.30 / 2.57 / 5.00 at period 150 and 2.00 / 1.31 / 4.95 at period 300,
above habituation alone on surprise (+0.90 \ci{-0.03}{+1.70} and +0.55
\ci{-0.07}{+1.14}, $p=0.19$ both) but well below the same interruption with
habituation (3.02 and 2.87). The two operations interact: the interruption
alone revives the stream only partly, because between two injections the
un-habituated model still falls into its orbits, and habituation alone
produces a fluent stream that goes nowhere; together they give the effect.
Two baselines say what habituation is not. A stronger penalty (1.3 instead
of 1.15) changes nothing (1.60 / 1.00 / 4.31; $\Delta$ 0.00 \ci{-0.67}{+0.60}
on surprise). Allowing the end-of-text token, so that the model may close its
document and open another (a boundary of its own choosing, on average every
few hundred tokens), gives more coherent text (6.12, +1.26 \ci{+0.07}{+2.28})
and no more surprise or connection (1.22 / 1.06; $-0.28$ and $-0.25$): the
model's own document breaks are boundaries without a subject, and they do
what a sham break does below. \todo{refresh with the complete battery 3}

\paragraph{Where the stream goes.} In sentence-embedding space
(Figure~\ref{fig:traj}) bare generation has explored radius 0.18 and mean step

to within 0.57 of the premise; the clock reseed spreads over the space in

\subsection{What carries the effect: boundary, content, context}
\label{sec:battery3}

An interruption is three things at once: a boundary in the text, a new
subject, and a context that is kept or dropped. Battery~3 separates them at
period 300 (Figure~\ref{fig:q3}, Table~\ref{tab:gen-q3}). A bare paragraph
break every 300 tokens (a sham interruption) leaves the stream where
habituation alone leaves it (1.67 / 0.80 / 4.33 vs 1.55 / 1.28 / 4.48). A
connective that asks for continuity (\emph{``And so, as before,''}) is worse
than nothing (0.67 / 0.56 / 3.46; surprise $-1.06$ \ci{-1.92}{-0.04}): a
boundary that points back closes the loop as the premise and own-past
injections did. The neutral subject change over the preserved context gives
2.87 / 2.38 / 6.43 (+1.32 \ci{+0.70}{+1.93} on surprise, $p=0.002$; +1.10 on
connection, $p=0.014$; +1.95 on coherence, $p=0.02$). And the same subject
change on a \emph{reset} context, where the model keeps only the premise and
the injected sentence, gives 3.17 / 3.31 / 7.10 (+1.44 \ci{+1.12}{+1.75}
surprise, +1.90 connection, +2.21 coherence): as high as, and on coherence
higher than, the preserved context. Resetting without a new subject (a break
on a reset context) gives 1.92 / 1.83 / 5.25, little more than the sham.
What carries the effect, then, is the new subject, with habituation; the
boundary by itself does nothing, and whether the earlier text stays in the
model's context or not makes no detectable difference to what the judge
reads. Two things must be said about that last result. Under reset the model
cannot integrate the earlier text, and its ``connection'' is what it can
still do: return to the premise, sometimes verbatim, in a fresh mini-story
that the judge, seeing 600 tokens of earlier text about the same premise,
scores as connected and coherent; under the preserved context the model can
integrate and returns less often, and the judge scores it about the same.
Connection as this rubric measures it does not separate a long-range
integration from a return to the shared beginning; the preserved context is
a choice of the recipe we studied, not a demonstrated ingredient of the
judged effect. \todo{refresh with the complete battery 3; confirmatory
replication (H3 pre-registered as clock300 $>$ reset on connection)}

\subsection{The interruption must lead away}
\label{sec:content}

With the same period (150), a neutral subject change and a re-encounter
stitch that asks the stream to return to its beginning are indistinguishable
(3.02 / 3.68 / 6.12 vs 2.83 / 3.70 / 5.50; every paired CI covers zero).
Injecting the \emph{premise itself} (1.16 / 1.11 / 3.91) or a 64-token window
of the stream's \emph{own past} (1.48 / 1.44 / 3.70) is as bad as not
interrupting at all: against the neutral change, surprise $-1.86$
\ci{-2.38}{-1.35} and $-1.54$ \ci{-2.17}{-0.86}, connection $-2.58$ and
$-2.24$, coherence $-2.21$ and $-2.42$, all $p\le0.006$, $\delta$ $-0.84$ to
$-1.00$ (Figure~\ref{fig:q2}). Qualitatively, the own-past injection
reinforces whatever mode the stream is in: an exam-key well was fed its own
questions back. A return works only as a short stitch that still opens a new
sentence; a literal return closes the loop.

\subsection{Timing: salience against the clock}
\label{sec:timing}

Salience events (jump, crystallization, recurrence; 1--9 per cell, median
$\approx$5) are the moments the scaffold's monitor picks out of the stream's
own telemetry. As a policy for \emph{which} windows to judge they beat a
clock in the first version of this study; the question here is whether they
are also the right moments to \emph{interrupt}. With the same stitch, timing
by salience events and timing by a clock of matched frequency (every 900
tokens) are indistinguishable on generated text: the post-interruption
windows score 3.04 / 2.92 / 5.75 against 3.05 / 2.71 / 5.72 (paired
differences $-0.01$ \ci{-0.76}{+0.73} on surprise, $+0.21$ on connection),
and the windows at least 300 tokens after the last injection, where the
stream is left alone, 1.82 / 1.80 / 4.20 against 2.08 / 1.71 / 4.73 ($-0.26$
\ci{-0.78}{+0.21}) (Figure~\ref{fig:q4}, Table~\ref{tab:gen-q4b}). Salience
monitoring by itself, with nothing injected, leaves the stream where
habituation alone leaves it (1.65 / 1.30 / 4.63 vs 1.55 / 1.28 / 4.48). The
first version of this study reported a large stream-level advantage of the
clock over salience timing (4.7 vs 2.2 on uniform windows); that advantage
was carried by the injected sentences inside the clock arm's windows, of
which it has more. What remains is a null at the present resolution:
salience is a good reader of the stream and, as a metronome, neither better
nor worse than a clock ticking at the same rate. Since a clock is free and
regular, the clock is what we recommend.

\subsection{Period and decay}
\label{sec:period}

The first version of this study reported that the yield of an interruption
grew with the length of the thread it broke (5.3--5.9 after 300--900-token
threads against 3.1 after 150) and decayed over the next few hundred tokens,
and named 300 tokens as the best rhythm. With the injected sentence excluded
from the judged window, that finding largely disappears
(Figure~\ref{fig:q5}). The post-interruption window (32--128 generated tokens
after the injection) scores 3.02 at period 150, 2.87 at 300, 2.88 at 600 and
3.18 at 900 on surprise, every paired difference against 150 covering zero;
connection is highest at the shortest period (3.68 vs 2.38, 2.02 and 2.22;
$\Delta$ $-1.30$ to $-1.67$, $p\le0.008$) and coherence rises a little with
the period (6.12 $\rightarrow$ 6.43, 6.25, 6.78). Deeper into a segment the
surprise of generated text holds for a few hundred tokens and then sinks
(period 900: 3.18, 2.67, 3.17, 2.77, 2.23 and 1.80 at 32, 160, 300, 450, 600
and 750 tokens after the injection, Figure~\ref{fig:q5b}), toward the level
of habituation alone (1.55). The stream estimate (offset means weighted by
the stretch of the segment they represent) is therefore 3.02 / 3.68 at 150,
2.76 / 2.30 at 300, 2.84 / 2.12 at 600 and 2.64 / 2.03 at 900
(Table~\ref{tab:gen-q5b}). Among the tested periods, then, no period is
better than 150 on the generated text itself, the shortest period gives the
most connection, and what the longer periods buy is a little coherence. A
period of 75 cannot be measured under this protocol (no 96-token window of
generated text fits between two injections); under the event protocol its
stream was dead (0.88), which is the one part of the earlier claim that
survives: a thread needs some tens of tokens to exist before it can be
broken. The larger numbers of the first version were, in good part, the judge
reading the injected sentence. What survives is the direction of every
effect and an operating range: a break every 150--300 tokens.

\subsection{Three generator models from two families}
\label{sec:families}

The ladder replicates on Qwen3-8B-Base and OLMo-2-13B (Table~\ref{tab:ladder},
Figure~\ref{fig:families}). On the 8B: 0.43 $\rightarrow$ 1.37 $\rightarrow$
2.76 (interruption over habituation: surprise +1.39 \ci{+0.72}{+2.07},
$p=0.008$; connection +2.17 \ci{+1.53}{+2.83}, $p=0.002$), scaffold 2.70 /
2.35. On OLMo, whose bare stream wanders across web genres rather than
locking into literal orbits and is therefore less dead (1.41), the
interruption over habituation gains connection (+1.51 \ci{+0.73}{+2.32},
$p=0.008$) and only some surprise (+0.67 \ci{-0.40}{+1.73}, $p=0.29$), and
costs nothing detectable in coherence; there the full scaffold is the best
arm on surprise (3.34, +1.20 over habituation, $p=0.08$) and coherence (6.30,
+1.95, $p=0.03$): its selective forgetting lifts the stream out of a
web-boilerplate well, the mechanism the calibration probes had suggested,
at the price of connection (2.04 vs 2.91). Whether forgetting is worth its
connection cost depends on how deep the generator's well is. \todo{refresh
OLMo numbers when its judging is complete}
